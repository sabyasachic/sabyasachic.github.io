Course Syllabus

Course Description

This course provides an introduction to the analysis of time series and other forms of sequential data. The course begins with regression methods for modeling trends and relationships over time and then develops methods specifically designed for dependent observations. Topics include multiple linear regression, nonlinear regression, regularized high-dimensional regression, variance modeling, spectral analysis, lagged regression, ARIMA models, and recurrent neural networks.

The emphasis throughout the course will be on understanding statistical models, fitting them to real datasets, quantifying uncertainty, and using them for prediction and forecasting.

Prerequisites

STAT 411 or equivalent background in probability and mathematical statistics is required. Prior exposure to regression, such as STAT 425, is strongly recommended.

Programming

Students may use R, Python, Julia, MATLAB, or another suitable programming language for homework assignments and projects. Classroom demonstrations will primarily use Python.

Assessment

There will be five homework assignments, one midterm examination, and a final examination.

The final course grade will be calculated using whichever of the following two schemes gives the student the higher score:

  • Homework: 30%, Midterm: 30%, Final Exam: 40%
  • Homework: 30%, Final Exam: 70%

The midterm is tentatively scheduled for the ninth week of the course, around October 21, 2026.

References

There is no required textbook for the course. Lecture notes, slides, code, and datasets will be posted on this website.

Useful references include:

  • Berkeley STAT 153 course materials on time series analysis.
