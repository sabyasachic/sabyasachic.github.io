Course Syllabus

Course Description

This graduate course develops statistical learning through the perspective of Bayesian statistics and probabilistic modeling. Rather than presenting statistical learning as a collection of unrelated algorithms, the course emphasizes a coherent framework connecting statistical inference, probability, computation, and modern machine learning.

Topics include regression and classification, cross-validation and bootstrap methods, Bayesian inference and empirical Bayes, regularization, nonlinear function estimation, Bayesian computation, variational inference, neural networks, and modern generative AI.

Course Topics

  • Bayesian Foundations and Supervised Learning: Bayesian philosophy, probabilistic modeling, prior and posterior distributions, linear regression, logistic regression, generalized linear models, and classification.
  • Model Assessment and Resampling: Cross-validation and bootstrap methods.
  • Bayesian Inference and Empirical Bayes: Conjugate models, Gaussian sequence models, empirical Bayes, James–Stein estimation, shrinkage, Dirichlet–multinomial models, and the Bayesian bootstrap.
  • Regularization and High-Dimensional Models: Ridge regression, Bayesian interpretations of regularization, lasso, and coordinate descent.
  • Nonlinear Function Estimation: Smoothing splines, local polynomial regression, Gaussian process regression, trend filtering, and isotonic regression.
  • Bayesian Computation: Monte Carlo methods, Metropolis–Hastings, Gibbs sampling, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, variational inference, ELBO optimization, and connections with the EM algorithm.
  • Modern Generative AI: Neural networks, latent variable models, variational autoencoders, diffusion models, and transformers.

The final project may be completed in groups of up to three students.
