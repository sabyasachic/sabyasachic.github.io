About Me

I received my Ph.D. in Statistics from Yale University, where my dissertation advisor was Professor Andrew Barron. I then joined the University of Chicago as a Kruskal Instructor, where I was fortunate to be mentored by Professor John Lafferty.

I am broadly interested in machine learning, statistics, probability, and related areas. Much of my research has been on the mathematics of curve fitting, technically known as nonparametric estimation. A recurring theme in my work is globally and locally adaptive estimation: recovering structured signals from noisy data using methods such as total variation denoising, trend filtering, isotonic regression, quantile regression, and other shape-constrained methods.
